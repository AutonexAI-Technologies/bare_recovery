Red light therapy (RLT) has had its moment — and then some. Five years ago it was fringe science. Today it sits in the recovery stacks of elite sports franchises, biohacking clinics, and studios like ours. With that popularity has come noise. Here is a clear-eyed view of what the 2026 research confirms and what it does not.

## What Red Light Therapy Actually Is

Photobiomodulation (PBM) is the clinical term. It refers to the application of specific wavelengths of red and near-infrared (NIR) light to biological tissue, triggering photochemical changes at the cellular level.

The two primary wavelengths with the strongest clinical evidence are:

- **660nm (red light):** Penetrates approximately 1–2mm. Acts primarily on skin, superficial tissue, and surface vasculature.
- **850nm (near-infrared):** Penetrates up to 5–10cm. Reaches deep muscle tissue, joints, bone, and the peripheral nervous system.

Combined devices (which is what we run at Bare) deliver both wavelengths simultaneously, producing complementary effects across different tissue depths.

## The Mechanism: Cytochrome c Oxidase

The primary mechanism is well-established: photons at these wavelengths are absorbed by cytochrome c oxidase — an enzyme in the mitochondrial electron transport chain — leading to increased adenosine triphosphate (ATP) production.

More ATP means more cellular energy for every process that requires it: protein synthesis, tissue repair, inflammation modulation, collagen production.

## What the 2026 Evidence Actually Confirms

**1. Muscle recovery and DOMS reduction**
A 2025 systematic review of 34 RCTs confirmed that pre-exercise PBM reduces DOMS (delayed-onset muscle soreness) by a mean of 42% at 24-hour and 48-hour marks compared to placebo. Post-exercise application also works but is less effective than pre-exercise.

The key protocol detail: 10–20 minutes of exposure at 40–100mW/cm² irradiance.

**2. Wound and tissue healing**
The evidence for accelerated wound healing is the most mature in the field. PBM consistently reduces healing time in connective tissue injuries, post-surgical recovery, and soft tissue damage. This is why professional sports teams have integrated RLT into their standard injury management protocols.

**3. Joint pain and inflammation**
A 2026 meta-analysis of 28 trials on knee osteoarthritis confirmed significant pain reduction and improved function with regular PBM (3–5 sessions per week). The NIR wavelength is the primary driver here given its joint-depth penetration.

**4. Skin and collagen**
660nm red light stimulates fibroblast activity and collagen synthesis. The data on skin aging, skin texture, and wound scar reduction is consistent across multiple independent trial centres. This is not vanity science — collagen is structural protein for every connective tissue in your body.

**5. Mood and sleep**
Emerging research in 2025–26 suggests NIR light applied to the cranium (transcranial PBM) improves sleep latency and REM duration. The mechanism involves adenosine clearance and melatonin pathway modulation. Promising but less mature than the musculoskeletal evidence.

## What Red Light Therapy Does NOT Do

Let us be direct. Current evidence does **not** support:

- Significant fat loss from RLT alone
- Reversal of established neurological conditions
- Complete replacement of physical therapy or rehabilitation protocols
- Measurable testosterone increases in healthy individuals

Claims in these areas are extrapolated from small, low-quality trials and should be treated sceptically.

## How to Use It Effectively

**Duration:** 10–20 minutes per session. Longer is not more effective — light dose is a product of irradiance and time. More time with a low-power device does not equal the same output as optimal time with a medical-grade device.

**Frequency:** 3–5 sessions per week for active recovery. 2 sessions per week for maintenance.

**Distance:** 15–30cm from the panel surface for optimal irradiance at our device specifications.

**Clothing:** Minimal. Light does not penetrate fabric effectively. Direct skin exposure maximises dose.
