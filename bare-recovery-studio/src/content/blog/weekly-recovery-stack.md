Recovery is not a single session. It is a system. The athletes who recover best are not the ones doing occasional sauna sessions — they are the ones who have built recovery into their weekly structure with the same deliberateness they bring to their training programme.

This is a practical framework for building a weekly recovery stack that works.

## The Foundation: Categorise Your Week

Before mapping recovery services to your week, identify your training pattern:

**High-volume weeks** (5–6 sessions): Maximum stress accumulation. Recovery becomes a performance necessity, not a luxury.

**Moderate weeks** (3–4 sessions): Standard training load. Recovery maintains readiness and prevents accumulation.

**Deload weeks** (1–2 sessions): Lower training stress. Active recovery accelerates adaptation from the previous load block.

Each category requires a different recovery volume — and a different service selection.

## Service Selection by Purpose

Understanding what each modality does best allows you to sequence them intelligently.

### Cold Plunge — Acute Inflammation & CNS Reset
**Best used:** Within 4–6 hours post-training (for performance recovery), or morning of a rest day (for CNS regulation and mood).
**Frequency:** 2–4 sessions per week.
**Avoid:** Immediately after hypertrophy-focused resistance training if muscle growth is the primary goal.

### Traditional / Infrared Sauna — Systemic Recovery & Sleep
**Best used:** Evening, 2–3 hours before bed. Heat exposure accelerates the body temperature drop that triggers deep sleep.
**Frequency:** 3–5 sessions per week is safe and beneficial. Even 2 sessions show measurable cardiovascular and recovery benefits.
**Pair with:** Cold plunge (contrast therapy) for amplified vascular pump effect.

### Contrast Therapy — The Power Protocol
**Best used:** After competitions, heavy training days, or back-to-back sessions.
**Frequency:** 1–3 sessions per week depending on training load.
**Protocol:** 3 rounds of 15 minutes sauna followed by 3 minutes cold plunge. Allow full vasoconstriction in cold before returning to heat.

### Red Light Therapy — Cellular Repair & Pre-Training Priming
**Best used:** Pre-workout (for performance priming — reduces DOMS and improves muscular output) or post-workout for tissue repair.
**Frequency:** 3–5 sessions per week for best results.
**Note:** RLT is additive — it does not conflict with other modalities and can be stacked before or after sauna/cold sessions.

### Compression Therapy — Lymphatic Flush & Leg Recovery
**Best used:** Post-training, especially after lower body sessions, long runs, or competitions.
**Frequency:** 2–4 sessions per week for active athletes.
**Timing:** Legs are freshest for the next session when compression is applied within 2 hours of training.

## A Sample Weekly Stack

Here is a practical weekly template for an athlete training 5 days per week:

**Monday (Training Day — Lower Body)**
→ Evening: Compression Therapy (20–30 min) + Red Light Therapy (15 min)

**Tuesday (Training Day — Upper Body)**
→ Evening: Sauna (20 min) + Cold Plunge (10 min)

**Wednesday (Rest Day)**
→ Morning: Contrast Therapy (full session, 45–60 min)

**Thursday (Training Day — Full Body)**
→ Post-training: Red Light Therapy (15 min)
→ Evening: Sauna (25 min)

**Friday (Training Day — Cardio/HIIT)**
→ Post-training: Cold Plunge (10 min) + Compression (20 min)

**Saturday (Light Training or Mobility)**
→ Evening: Sauna (30 min)

**Sunday (Full Rest)**
→ Morning or afternoon: Full Circuit (all 6 services, 60–90 min)

This structure delivers approximately **7–9 recovery service touchpoints per week** — well within the range that research identifies as optimal for serious training loads.

## The Compounding Effect

The value of a recovery stack is not linear. Each individual session has a measurable effect. But the compound effect of consistent recovery across 8–12 weeks is qualitatively different:

- Reduced baseline muscle tension
- Faster return to baseline after hard training days
- Improved sleep quality (measurable on HRV)
- Lower resting cortisol
- Reduced injury rate from overuse stress

These are not subjective impressions. They are consistently reported across athlete cohorts who maintain structured recovery programmes.

## The Minimum Effective Dose

If a full weekly stack is not feasible, the minimum effective dose for meaningful benefit is:

**2 sessions per week:** Contrast therapy twice per week. This single protocol, done consistently, will produce the broadest systemic benefit across the most physiological pathways.

**1 session per week:** Full Circuit. One session covering all 6 modalities is the highest-leverage single touchpoint available.
